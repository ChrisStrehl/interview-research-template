# Lean Canvas, filled from the outside

Nine boxes, one page, the business model as the company appears to run it. Ash Maurya's layout, kept
because a hiring manager recognises it at a glance.

| Box | What goes in | Where it comes from | What you usually cannot see |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem** | The top three problems the company says it solves, then the one users actually praise it for | business.md, users.md | Whether the stated problem is the one the roadmap serves |
| **Customer segments** | Who pays, who uses, and the early adopter the marketing is aimed at. For two-sided products, both sides, and which side pays | business.md, users.md, market.md | Segment mix by revenue |
| **Unique value proposition** | Their own headline, verbatim, and the one users repeat back in reviews | business.md, users.md, wayback comparison | Whether it changed and why |
| **Solution** | The three product capabilities that carry the value, named as the product names them | product-map.md | Roadmap |
| **Channels** | How users arrive: stores, SEO, paid, partnerships, sales. Store rankings and referral mechanics if observed | market.md, product-map.md, users.md | Channel mix and cost |
| **Revenue streams** | Pricing model, tiers, who is billed, take rate or spread if a marketplace | business.md | ARR, mix, churn |
| **Cost structure** | The visible big costs: team size, paid acquisition, payouts, infrastructure, sales org | money.md, people.md | Actual numbers; mark [I] with the working |
| **Key metrics** | What the company itself measures in public (users, payouts, GMV, seats) and what its analytics calls suggest it tracks | business.md, evidence/product/network.md | Internal north star |
| **Unfair advantage** | What could not be copied quickly: data, supply relationships, brand, distribution deals, regulatory position. Be strict; most companies have none and that is a finding | market.md, money.md | Whether they know it |

## Rules

- Three to five lines per box, each ending in `[grade] file.md`.
- The **Problem** box is the one most often filled from marketing copy. Prefer what reviews say the
  product is used for over what the site says it is for; the gap between the two is a finding for the
  point of view.
- **Revenue** and **Cost** are where inference concentrates. Show the arithmetic once, in the box.
- If **Unfair advantage** is honestly empty, write "None observed [U]; positioning rests on X" and
  move on. Do not decorate it.
